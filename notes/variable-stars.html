Variable stars and Cepheids

Some stars change in brightness. How they change tells us what they are, and one kind tells us how far away it is.

Why Cepheids vary

A Cepheid is a giant star that swells and shrinks, getting brighter and dimmer as it pulsates.

Measuring distance with a Cepheid

The longer a Cepheid's period, the more luminous it is. So its period gives its absolute magnitude M, and comparing M with its average apparent magnitude m gives its distance, using the data sheet formula M = m + 5 − 5 log d (see Magnitude: how bright is a star?).

Worked example: a Cepheid in the Large Magellanic Cloud has period 10 days, so the graph gives M ≈ −4.0, and average m = +14.5. Then m − M = 18.5, 5 log d = 23.5, log d = 4.7 and d ≈ 50,000 pc. Reading M as −4.2 to −3.8 gives 45,700 to 55,000 pc, so the uncertainty is half that range, about ±4,600 pc.
