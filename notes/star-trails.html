Star trails and the sidereal day

Leave a camera pointing at the night sky, and the stars draw arcs: a record of the Earth turning.

What a star trail is

A star trail image is a long exposure: the camera stays still while the Earth turns, so each star draws an arc around the . Drag the exposure time and watch the arcs grow.

A drawn image, not a photograph. That stars circle the celestial pole, near the Pole Star in the northern sky: first draft, to be checked by the teacher.

Measure the angle, find the sidereal day

In the exposure time t, the Earth turns through the angle θ that every arc covers. One full turn, 360°, relative to the stars is a sidereal day. So:

sidereal day = (360 ÷ θ) × t

Line the two arms up with the two ends of the marked arc, and read θ.

How sure is the answer?

Five students read the same arc. Their readings spread a little, so the uncertainty is half the range of the readings. Repeating a reading shows that spread, shows up an anomaly to leave out, and gives a mean; it does not make the method itself more accurate. A longer exposure gives a bigger angle for the same reading spread, so the percentage uncertainty is smaller.

The readings are made up, each within about ±1° of the true angle, read to the nearest 0.5°.

Why not exactly 24 hours?

Star trails time the Earth's turn against the stars. As it turns, the Earth also moves along its orbit, so after one turn against the stars it has to turn a little further to face the Sun again. A solar day is that little bit longer.

That the Earth moves about 1° along its orbit each day, so the extra turn takes about 4 minutes: first draft, to be checked by the teacher. The sidereal and solar days are the data sheet's. More on Star Coordinates.

Can star trails tell the two days apart?

Only if the measurement's range is narrower than the few minutes between them. A longer measurement gives a smaller percentage uncertainty; repeating the same reading does not.

A whole night of about 720 minutes (12 hours, a long winter night), read with the same spread as the 240-minute readings: first draft, to be checked by the teacher.
