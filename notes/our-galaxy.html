Our Galaxy

We live inside the Milky Way, so we see it from within: as a band across the sky.

The Milky Way from Earth

On a dark, moonless night, away from street lights, a faint band of light arches across the sky:

Through binoculars or a small telescope, the band breaks up into countless faint stars, with star clusters and glowing gas clouds. The dark lanes are clouds of dust blocking the starlight behind them. We see a band because we are inside a flat disc of stars, looking along it. More naked-eye sights on The Naked-Eye Sky.

Shape, size and type

From outside, our Galaxy would look like this. Switch between the two views:

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy, type SBb: a bar of stars across the centre, with spiral arms winding out from its ends. New stars form in the arms, where the gas and dust are. Old globular clusters (such as M13) orbit in the halo, a round region all round the disc. At the very centre is a supermassive black hole.

About 100,000 light-years across, the disc about 1,000 light-years thick, the Sun about 26,000 light-years from the centre, about 150 globular clusters, and the bar and arms drawn from a simple model: first draft, to be checked by the teacher.

Mapping it with 21 cm radio waves

Dust in the disc blocks visible light, so we can see only a few thousand light-years along it (more on seeing through dust). But cold hydrogen gas, spread right through the disc, gives out radio waves 21 cm long, which pass through dust (see Radio telescopes).

The gas goes round the centre. Gas moving away from us shifts the 21 cm line to longer wavelengths (redshift); gas moving towards us, to shorter (blueshift). Each arm the line of sight crosses moves at its own speed, so it makes its own peak:

Knowing how fast the gas goes round, each peak's speed gives how far away that arm is. Done in every direction, the peaks map the arms and show the Galaxy's rotation.

The 21 cm line (21.1 cm), seeing a few thousand light-years in visible light, every orbit taken as about 220 km/s, and the speeds from this simple model: first draft, to be checked by the teacher.
