The balance, stage by stage

As in the Sun, gravity pulls inward and pressure pushes outward. Here both are far stronger, and when fusion stops at iron, the outward push fails.

In a supernova the iron core collapses in under a second. If what is left is held up by neutron pressure (neutrons squeezed so close they resist any more), it is a neutron star. If it is too heavy even for that, it collapses for good into a black hole.

Two paths across the HR diagram

Both stars start on the main sequence, but the massive star starts far higher up: hotter and much more luminous. It then moves right across the top to become a red supergiant, while a Sun-like star moves up and right to the red giants, then down to the white dwarfs.
