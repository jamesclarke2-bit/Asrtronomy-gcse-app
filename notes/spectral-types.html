Colour, temperature and spectral type

A star's colour and the lines in its spectrum both depend on how hot its surface is, so astronomers sort stars into seven spectral classes by temperature.

Why colour tells you temperature

Three stars' glow curves, each scaled to the same height, against the visible band.

A hotter star gives out more of its light at shorter wavelengths, so across the visible band its blue end is brighter and it looks bluer. A cooler star gives out more of its light at longer wavelengths, so it looks redder. Colour is a thermometer you can read from trillions of kilometres away.
