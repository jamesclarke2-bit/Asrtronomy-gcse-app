Galaxy types

Edwin Hubble sorted galaxies by their shape. Can you?

Four kinds of galaxy

The Hubble classification puts every galaxy in one of four classes: spiral (a round bulge with arms), barred spiral (a straight bar through the centre, the arms starting from its ends), elliptical (a smooth oval, no arms) and irregular (no regular shape). Sort these:

Hubble's tuning fork

Hubble laid the classes out like a tuning fork:

Ellipticals sit along the handle, from round E0 to the flattest E7. The handle splits at S0, a disc with no arms. Spirals run along one prong and barred spirals along the other, from a (big bulge, tightly wound arms) to c (small bulge, loose arms). Irregulars don't fit, so they sit apart. The Milky Way is SBb.

Hubble once wondered whether galaxies change along the fork, from left to right; that is no longer thought true, as the fork only sorts galaxies by shape. That idea, what S0 and the E numbers and a to c mean, and Hubble as the one who drew it: first draft, to be checked by the teacher.

Exam tips
