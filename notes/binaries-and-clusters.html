Binaries, clusters and the view through a telescope

Many stars are not alone: they come in pairs and in clusters, held together by gravity.

Double star or binary?

Two pairs of stars that look the same in the sky can be very different in space.

  • Visual binary: both stars can be seen through a telescope, and measurements over years show them moving around each other.
  • Spectroscopic binary: too close to split, but the spectral lines shift back and forth as each star moves towards us and away (see Reading a spectrum).
  • Eclipsing binary: seen edge-on, each star passes in front of the other, so the total brightness dips regularly. Eclipsing binaries: coming soon.
