Active galaxies

Some galaxies pour out radio waves, X-rays and more from a tiny, brilliant centre.

A galaxy with an active centre

Some galaxies give out huge amounts of radiation beyond visible light: radio waves, infrared, X-rays and gamma rays. It comes from a tiny region at the centre, an Active Galactic Nucleus (AGN):

An AGN is powered by matter falling onto a supermassive black hole. Gas spirals in through an accretion disc, heated to millions of degrees, and gives out vast energy before it falls in; some is fired out along two jets. More on Black holes: the evidence and X-ray accretion discs.

Seyferts, quasars and blazars

Turn the view, and change the power:

A Seyfert galaxy is a fairly nearby spiral with an unusually bright centre. A quasar is so luminous and distant that it looks like a star. A blazar has a jet pointing almost straight at us. Quasars were first found by radio telescopes.
