Why the Sun, Moon and planets stay near it

The Solar System formed from one flattened, spinning disc of gas and dust, so the planets still orbit close to that same plane — seen from Earth, that plane traces the ecliptic, and every planet wanders close to it. The Moon's own orbit is tilted only about 5° to it, so the Moon stays close too.

Astronomy's constellations vs astrology's star signs

Astronomy uses the 88 official, unequal-sized IAU constellations — which is why the ecliptic actually crosses thirteen of them, Ophiuchus included. Astrology instead uses twelve equal 30° "signs" with fixed calendar dates that haven't been updated for precession's slow drift.

Exam tips

Common exam confusion: an astrological "star sign" and an astronomical constellation are not the same thing. Astrology's twelve signs are equal 30° slices with fixed dates; astronomy's real constellations are unequal widths, there are thirteen of them along the ecliptic (Ophiuchus included), and the Sun's actual constellation on a given date can disagree with the "sign" for that date.
