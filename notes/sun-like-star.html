What holds a star steady

Gravity pulls every part of a star inward. In a main-sequence star, in the core releases energy, and the it creates pushes outward. When the two are equal, the star stays the same size.

The balance changes through the star's life. A protostar shrinks because gravity wins. A red giant's core shrinks while its outer layers swell. A white dwarf has no fusion at all: it is held up by electron pressure, because its electrons are squeezed so close together that they resist being squeezed any closer.

Its path across the HR diagram

Plotted on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram, the star moves as it changes: onto the main sequence, up and right as a red giant, then left and down to become a white dwarf.
