The Moon is always half-lit on the side facing the Sun — that never changes. What changes as it orbits is how much of that lit half we can see from Earth.

Waxing or waning

Common exam confusion: phases are caused by the Moon's changing position relative to the Sun — and so how much of its permanently half-lit surface faces Earth — not by Earth's shadow falling on the Moon. Earth's shadow on the Moon is a completely separate, much rarer event: a , which only happens when the Sun, Earth and Moon line up almost exactly — unlike an ordinary phase, which happens every single day of every month.
