Retrograde Motion and Planetary Alignments

Mars orbits the Sun more slowly than Earth does — so roughly every 26 months, Earth "overtakes" it on the inside track, and Mars briefly appears to move backwards against the background stars. Watch it happen below, and see how conjunction, opposition and elongation are just different stages of the same repeating cycle.

Mars's apparent position over time

Mars's position projected against the background stars, plotted across the whole date range above. The line climbs steadily most of the time (Mars drifting eastward through the stars, as expected) but briefly bends backwards around each — that backward-curving stretch is the retrograde loop. It repeats once every (about 780 days, roughly 26 months) — this graph spans exactly two of them, so you can see the pattern repeat.
