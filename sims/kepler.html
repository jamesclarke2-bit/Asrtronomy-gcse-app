Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

Three rules that describe every orbit: the shape (an ellipse with the Sun at one focus), the changing speed around it (faster near the Sun, slower far away), and how period and distance relate across the whole Solar System. Drag the sliders, watch the body move, and see the straight-line graph that falls out of the third law — try the presets below to jump from Earth's gentle orbit to a comet's wildly elongated one.

3. Third law: T² against r³

Planets (exam data sheet)   Comets (not on the exam data sheet)

Every planet sits close to the same straight line through the origin — that line is Kepler's third law, T² = r³ (T in years, r in AU), for anything orbiting the Sun. Plotted points use the exam data sheet's own rounded distance and period figures, so a point can sit very slightly off the line — that's the data sheet's own rounding, not an error.

Presets

One full animated sweep always represents one full orbit of whatever body is currently selected — a few seconds on screen whether that orbit really takes one year (Earth) or 2.8 million years (the long-period comet). Only the semi-major axis changes the real period (Kepler's third law); eccentricity alone never does.

The orbit view and second law diagrams rescale automatically to fit whichever orbit is selected, so a comet's enormous orbit and Earth's tiny one can look about the same size here — only the AU figures tell you the real difference.
