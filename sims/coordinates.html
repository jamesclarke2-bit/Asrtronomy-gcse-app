Find your latitude using Polaris

Polaris sits so close to the north celestial pole that, to within about a degree, its altitude is your latitude — whatever the time, date or hour angle. Measure Polaris's altitude with a sextant or protractor, and you've measured your latitude.

Diurnal motion

As Earth rotates, the selected star's altitude rises and falls through the day, always peaking at hour angle 0 (transit). Drag anywhere on the graph to scrub through the hour angle and see the sky diagram above follow along.
