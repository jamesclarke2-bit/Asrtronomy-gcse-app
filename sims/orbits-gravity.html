Orbits and Gravity

Why do planets stay in orbit? Gravity pulls with a force that gets weaker with the square of the distance — by the end of this page you should be able to use those ratios (double the distance, quarter the force) and explain why a fast enough sideways fall becomes a stable orbit instead of a crash.

1. Inverse-square law

The same pull spreads over more area the further out it goes — 4 times the area at twice the distance, 9 times at three times the distance, so each square gets a quarter, then a ninth, of the force.
