Sidereal vs Synodic Month

"How long does the Moon take to orbit Earth?" has two different correct answers, depending on what you measure its position against: the distant, effectively-fixed background stars, or the Sun.

Common exam confusion: the Moon's orbital period is not a single number. The (~27.3 days) is measured against the fixed stars; the (~29.5 days) is measured against the Sun, and is what determines the cycle of phases — that's the one to use for "how long between one Full Moon and the next".

Why the gap?

Watch the top diagram while you scrub or animate: Earth doesn't stand still while the Moon orbits it — it's moving along its own orbit around the Sun at the same time. By the time the Moon has swept all the way round to point at the reference star again (~27.3 days), Earth has travelled a small extra arc of its own orbit — and that shifts the Sun's true direction, as seen from Earth, by that same small amount.

The Moon has finished its lap against the stars, but the Sun has "moved on" a little in the meantime, so the Moon hasn't yet caught back up to the same Sun-Earth-Moon alignment that defines a repeating phase. It needs roughly 2.2 extra days of travel to close that gap — which is exactly the difference between the two months: 29.53 − 27.32 ≈ 2.2 days.
